# TITLE: The Paper That Cheated
# FORMAT: A, curious friend
# LENGTH: about 45 s, 104 spoken words
# QUESTION: Does the paper lose because it is lighter than the book?
# ANSWERED AT: "The book shields the paper from most of that resistance."
# CAST: berry, benny
# PLACE: classroom
# TONE: Playful argument; Benny is curious, not foolish, and Berry lets the result do the persuading.
# DEMONSTRATION: Use a closed, broad book and a loose sheet smaller than its cover; release them without pushing.

# SEE: Benny holds a flat sheet and a horizontal book side by side at the same height.
benny: Heavy things always land first. Watch.
berry: Always?
benny: The book's heavier. Easy victory.

# SEE: Both are released together; the book lands while the paper flutters down.
benny: Look at that. The paper's barely trying.
berry: Same paper. Same book. Another race.
benny: You can encourage it. It still won't win.

# SEE: Berry places the loose sheet flat on top of the book.
benny: That's not a race. That's public transport.
berry: If weight alone decides, the book should leave it behind.
benny: Fine. But no glue.
berry: No glue. No tricks. Just watch.

# SEE: Berry releases the level book with the paper on top, and they fall together.
benny: It kept up! You didn't make it heavier.
berry: Air was slowing the loose sheet down.
benny: So what changed?
berry: The book shields the paper from most of that resistance.
benny: So being light wasn't the whole explanation?
berry: Exactly.

# SEE: Benny looks from the fallen book to his schoolbag.
benny: Can it give my homework a lift, too?

# FACTS TO CHECK
# - Verified: A flat loose sheet falls more slowly than a book in the side-by-side demonstration because air resistance affects their motion differently.
# - Verified: A sheet resting flat on a broader falling book can remain with it because the book largely shields it from oncoming air.
# - The paper's mass does not change between trials; weight alone does not explain the different results.
# - Source: Simon Fraser University Physics, "1C20.11 Book and Paper Drop": https://www.sfu.ca/physics/demos/demos-experiments/book-paper-burnaby.html
# - Do not animate the book pulling or gluing the sheet downward, or claim that this is a precise measurement of gravitational acceleration.
