# TITLE: The Ribbon That Talked
# FORMAT: D, one-minute mystery
# LENGTH: about 60 s, 135 spoken words
# QUESTION: Which friend opened the present?
# ANSWERED AT: "And your pocket has the missing piece."
# CAST: berry, benny, cherry
# PLACE: living room
# TONE: A fair, low-stakes detective comedy; curiosity gets Benny into trouble.
# CLUE: A broad gold ribbon fragment with one large zigzag edge protrudes from Benny's hoodie pocket in his first shot.
# FAIR PLAY: Keep the fragment visible at normal viewing size before the accusation; do not point at, spotlight, hide, or introduce it only at the reveal.
# CONTINUITY: The same unique zigzag edge matches the torn end on the gift; no other gold ribbon appears in the scene.

# SEE: Berry holds a gift box whose wide gold ribbon has a conspicuously torn end.
berry: Who opened this while I was gone?
benny: Before anyone says anything, I respect privacy.
cherry: That's a very specific opening statement.

# SEE: Benny stands with the gold fragment protruding from his pocket while Cherry holds scissors beside an opened snack bag.
berry: I left you two alone for one whole minute.
cherry: I stayed here. Benny left for water, then came back.
benny: Which proves I was busy doing something completely normal.
berry: You both had time. Did anybody else come in?
cherry: No. Just us.
benny: Cherry has scissors. Case closed.
cherry: For my snack bag.
benny: That's exactly what somebody with scissors would say.

# SEE: Berry holds the torn ribbon end beside Cherry's scissors without cutting anything.
berry: This wasn't a clean cut. Somebody pulled it apart.
cherry: Thank you.
benny: Could've been a very determined breeze.
berry: With the windows shut?
benny: An indoor breeze. Rare, but ambitious.

# SEE: Berry points to the gold fragment already protruding from Benny's pocket.
berry: And your pocket has the missing piece.
benny: That's... decorative.

# SEE: Berry holds the fragment beside the ribbon, aligning their matching zigzag edges.
cherry: It fits exactly.
benny: I wanted to know if you'd remembered my birthday.
berry: It's tomorrow. I remembered.

# SEE: Benny looks down at his pocket while Berry holds the matched ribbon up beside him.
berry: Next time, wrap your evidence too.

# FACTS TO CHECK
# - Original fictional deduction, not a claim of forensic certainty in real cases.
# - The scissors are a plausible distraction, not proof; the matching unique ribbon fragment is the decisive fictional evidence.
