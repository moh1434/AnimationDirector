# Series plan: a funny family that thinks wrong in ways we recognize

Status: **approved by the user on 2026-11-25.** Steps 1–3 are done. `FAMILY_BIBLE.md` and `RULES.md` are now the working documents; where they differ from this plan, they win.

Goal, in this order: 1) make people laugh, 2) make them care about this family, 3) leave them able to spot one thinking mistake.

## 1. What went wrong so far

| Version | What it did | Why it failed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demo scripts (`episodes demo 1.x`) | Someone says a fallacy, Wendy corrects it, the lesson is spoken | A lesson wearing a story. The mistake could belong to anyone, and Wendy is always right, so there is no surprise |
| The Long Way Home, and episodes 1–3 | Warm family moments | Nothing to learn, few laughs, and the viewer never learns how this life began |
| `RULES.md` today | Keeps fallacies out of sight and lets an episode have none | It swung from "all lesson" to "no lesson" |

The best idea was in the demos: Benny burns muffins on purpose because the café filled up after he burned one. **His bad reasoning caused
the funny action.** That is the show.

## 2. What the research says, and what we do with it

| Finding | What we do |
| --- | --- |
| Learning from a story depends on how close the lesson is to the plot (Fisch's capacity model) [1]. Mostly studied on children's TV | **The mistake is the plot.** Remove the mistake and no story is left |
| *The Good Place* mixed philosophy with comedy. Its creator warns that a wrong move in a plot-driven show loses people, and says they moved toward the richness of the characters [2] | Characters first. No teacher and no speeches: an event breaks the mistake, not an explanation |
| Videos under two minutes helped people recognize false dilemmas, ad hominem and similar tricks; on YouTube recognition rose about 5% [4] | One mistake per Short is realistic. Expect small gains per episode, so repeat each idea in new situations |
| One training video reduced biases right away and two months later; practice with feedback worked better [5] | A short name card at the end, and regular "Spot it" quiz episodes |
| Critical thinking transfers poorly to situations that look different [6]; comparing a wrong and a right example looks promising [7] | Bring every mistake back with another character in another setting. Sometimes show good reasoning that *looks* like the mistake |
| Humor tied to the idea tends to help; unrelated or sarcastic humor mostly doesn't; results are mixed overall [8] | The biggest laugh **is** the mistake. Never joke first and teach later |
| Story-based campaigns use three kinds of characters, and the one who changes is the one viewers copy; the evidence is mostly from its supporters [9] | Everyone is sometimes wrong. Cherry is the one who visibly learns |
| *BoJack Horseman*: the writers deliberately give each character negative traits [10]. Its creator wants us to root for BoJack without glorifying him, and his past never cancels what he does [3] | Every flaw is also a strength. It explains the behavior and never excuses it, and consequences last. Our tone stays light and family-safe |
| *The Big Bang Theory*'s first pilot failed because audiences disliked the rough female lead; they rewrote her as kind and charmed by the group [11]. One critic argues the characters matter more there than the story structure [12] | Four *ways of thinking* collide over one event. Teasing is fine, contempt is not. They always like each other |
| Screenwriter John August warns that networks dislike pilots that only set things up; "one new guy" joining an existing group works well [13] | Episode 1 shows the event that starts this life, but it must be as funny as episode 5. The delivery guy is our newcomer |
| YouTube ranks Shorts by how many choose to watch, how long and what share they watch [14] | A funny, clear problem in the first 3 seconds, and every episode works alone |

Limits: I opened sources 2, 3, 10, 12 and 13 and they support the wording above. The rest come from search summaries and abstracts; 1 could not be
opened. I found no study of a sitcom in Shorts that teaches fallacies, so part of this is our own bet. The character details in section 4 are my
design, not research.

## 3. The show

**Working title:** *It Made Sense at the Time*

**One line:** A loving family in which everyone is smart in one way and confidently wrong in another, and each day somebody's perfectly
reasonable idea makes a mess.

**Promise to the viewer:** you will laugh at the idea, then recognize it as yours.

### The engine (every episode, about 60–100 seconds)

1. **Hook, 0–3 s.** The strangest result or claim, already happening. "Great news! I quit my job!"
2. **The reason.** Why it makes sense to this person. It must be *tempting*: half the viewers should nod.
3. **It grows.** They act on it. Somebody copies it or fights it with a mistake of their own.
4. **Reality answers.** Something happens that the idea can't survive. Best of all, they meet their own logic in another mouth.
5. **Button.** They pay for it, laugh about it, or start it again in a new form. Last line is a joke.
6. **Name card, 2–3 s, silent.** The mistake's name and one plain sentence. Nobody in the story ever says the name.

### Rules that keep it funny

- **No one is the teacher.** Whoever spots the mistake changes every episode, and nobody is always right.
- **Nobody is stupid.** Every mistake has a real feeling under it.
- **One mistake per episode**, marked and checked. Other characters may do small ones for color, but only one gets the card.
- **The weak reason and the wrong conclusion are different things.** A bad argument can reach a true answer; sometimes let it.
- **A family scene, not a debate.** At most one line may explain, and it must also be a joke or hurt.
- **It must work in Arabic.** No puns or wordplay that exist only in English. The name card is translated too (`translations.json`).
- **Feeling gets five seconds.** One honest moment per episode, never skipped and never a speech.

## 4. The characters

Each one is a way of thinking. Strength and flaw are the same trait.

| | Benny | Berry | Wendy | Cherry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Thinks with** | Hope | Stories | Proof | Deals |
| **Motto they'd never say aloud** | If it feels right, it's right | I can see exactly what this means | If you can't measure it, it isn't there | There's always a way to yes |
| **Strength** | Tries things, forgives fast | Notices what others miss | Checks | Asks what nobody dares ask |
| **Same trait as a flaw** | Counts the hits, forgets the misses | Fills gaps with a story and believes it | Thinks she is the only one without bias | Bends any rule toward what she wants |
| **Goes wrong with** | Survivorship bias, false cause, sunk cost, planning fallacy, gambler's fallacy, hot hand | Confirmation bias, mind reading, false dilemma, slippery slope, appeal to emotion | Bias blind spot, overconfidence, fallacy fallacy, false precision, curse of knowledge | Bandwagon, special pleading, whataboutism, moving goalposts, loaded question |
| **Wants** | To be the one who made it work | To be understood without explaining | To be right | To be counted as a full person |
| **Needs** | To be loved on a day it didn't work | To ask instead of guess | To be wrong and still be fine | To say plainly what she wants |
| **When caught** | Jokes, then goes quiet | Defends, then says it straight | Corrects your wording | Renegotiates |
| **Running bit** | Announces good news that isn't | "I'm not upset" (she has already cleaned a whole room) | Fridge charts | Written contracts in crayon |

**The delivery guy: reality, on a motorcycle.** He never speaks. When he arrives, the consequence arrives with him. Sometimes a look at him is
the punchline. He is also our newcomer: he sees the family as we do.

**Pairs that make comedy:** Hope + Proof (Benny, Wendy). Story + Deal (Berry, Cherry). Hope + Story (the marriage: one jumps, the other
imagines the landing). Proof + Deal (the sisters: evidence against lawyering).

**What lasts between episodes** (a little, as in the shows you like): Benny's restaurant slowly lives or dies, Berry learns to sell what she loves,
Wendy becomes easier to live with, Cherry turns into the best thinker in the house. A viewer who has seen nothing still understands
each episode.

**Ages:** Benny 41, Berry 39, Wendy 18, Cherry 10.

**Wendy** has no job for now: she is taking a gap year before college.

## 5. Where the story starts

Your point is right: the audience must see the restaurant being born. So we start **the day Benny quits**.
It introduces everyone through one question (what do you do when Dad quits?), gives the series its stakes, and its mistake is survivorship
bias, the mistake behind a thousand "follow your dream" videos.

### Season 1 (10 episodes)

| # | Title | Who is wrong | Mistake · field | The comedy | What breaks it | What we learn about the family |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Every Famous Chef** | Benny | Survivorship bias · statistics | He quits because every famous chef once quit | Cherry quits school for the same reason | Who they are, and that the house needs money |
| 2 | **Two Names** | Berry, Benny | False dilemma · fallacy | Naming the restaurant becomes "mine or yours" | Cherry has already opened the page under a third name | The page opens; how they fight and make up |
| 3 | **Four Out of Four** | Benny | Biased sample · statistics | His survey proves everyone loves the hot sauce: he asked his family | The delivery guy takes one bite | They lie to be kind; nobody has ordered yet |
| 4 | **The Socks** | Benny, then all | False cause · fallacy | The first order came while he wore odd socks, so now no one may wash anything | Orders arrive while the socks are in the machine | First order; they worry about money together |
| 5 | **One Star** | Benny | Negativity bias · cognitive bias | Nineteen good reviews, one bad one, and he hunts the reviewer | The nineteen happy people's orders go cold while he hunts. (Button: the reviewer is a small child who thought one star meant first place) | Praise doesn't reach him |
| 6 | **Forty Hours** | Berry | Sunk cost · decision making | Her horse painting looks like a potato, and she can't stop because she's spent forty hours | She hears herself tell Benny to throw away a ruined soup | She can't let go of what she made |
| 7 | **The Chart** | Wendy | Bias blind spot · cognitive bias | She charts everyone's mistakes on the fridge and leaves her own column out | All day the family quietly fills it in for her | Being right is lonely; her first apology |
| 8 | **Everyone Has One** | Cherry | Bandwagon · fallacy | Everyone in class has a phone, so she must have one | Her parents use it back: everyone in class is in bed by eight. "I'm not everyone!" | She wants to be reachable when her parents work |
| 9 | **The Blind Taste** | All four | Expectation effect · scientific thinking | All are sure they can tell Benny's sauce from the shop's | Both cups hold the same sauce | Benny asks for a fair test of himself, for the first time |
| 10 | **The Old Job** | Nobody, for once | A fair comparison · decision making | His old boss wants him back, and the family "helps" using every mistake of the season | Benny hears the quiet ones this time | He decides well, and it still scares him |

**Spot it** (after 5 and 10, then every 5): a 30–40 s quiz Short. Three new lines from the family: which mistake is it? Practice matters, so these aren't
recaps.

**Contrast episodes:** now and then, reasoning that only looks like the mistake. Example: the smoke alarm rings after Benny cooks, and this time it
*is* the cooking.

### Ideas already fitting the other fields

| Field | Seeds |
| --- | --- |
| Logical fallacies | Slippery slope (Cherry gets one cat sticker) · Ad hominem ("you can't cook, so you can't judge") · Straw man (a fight about dishes) · Appeal to authority (a TV doctor on knives) |
| Critical thinking | Who pays the food blogger? · The viral tip no one checked · A true photo with a false caption |
| Cognitive biases | Anchoring (Berry prices a painting) · Availability (a shark film before the beach) · Halo effect (a charming customer who never pays) |
| Logical reasoning | If it rains orders rise; orders rose; so did it rain? · All chefs taste first, so everyone who tastes is a chef |
| Probability, statistics | Gambler's fallacy (five tails in a row) · Regression to the mean (the lucky day) · Average versus typical ("our average customer has 1.4 dogs") |
| Scientific thinking | Change one thing at a time · What would prove me wrong? · A test with nothing to compare to |
| Decision making | Planning fallacy ("twenty minutes") · Opportunity cost (the huge order) · Loss aversion (the coupon that must be used) |

## 6. Proof of tone: the opening of episode 1

A sample, not an approved script. No narrator.

```text
# SEE: The front door flies open. Benny holds a cardboard box with a frying pan sticking out.
benny: Great news! I quit my job!

# SEE: Berry's paintbrush stops. Cherry's spoon stops. Wendy keeps typing.
berry: The job that pays for the house?
benny: Chef Marlo quit his job. Famous! Chef Tilda quit hers. Famous!
benny: Every famous chef quit their job!
wendy: Did you read that on a mug?
benny: On two mugs.

cherry: So famous people quit things.
benny: Exactly!
cherry: Great. I quit school.
benny: What? No!
cherry: The lady who made my game quit school. Famous!
benny: That's one lady! Think of all the kids who quit, and you never even...

# SEE: Benny stops. Hold for two seconds.
benny: ...hear about them.

# SEE: Berry puts down her brush.
berry: Call your boss.
benny: I may have sung on the way out.
berry: Sung what?
wendy: It's online. There's choreography.

# SEE: Nobody speaks. Benny's eyes move slowly to the stove.
berry: No.
benny: I didn't say anything.
berry: You looked at my kitchen.

# CARD: SURVIVORSHIP BIAS: the winners tell their story. The others are too quiet to hear.
```

## 7. Two checks before any script is approved

**Funny:** Would a stranger laugh with the sound off at least once? Is the biggest laugh the mistake itself? Does each person speak so that you could
name them without the label? Is there a surprise in the last ten seconds? Read aloud: cut any line that only explains.

**Useful:** Remove the mistake: does the plot collapse? Is it tempting? Is it the right name for what happens (checked against a reliable source)?
Could the viewer spot it tomorrow somewhere else? Did an event, not a person, prove it wrong?

3. **Rewrite the foundation.** `FAMILY_BIBLE.md` from section 4, `RULES.md` from sections 3 and 7, and an empty `SERIES_LOG.md`.
4. **Write episode 1 three ways** (three different jokes and endings). You choose or mix. This sets the show's voice.
5. **Write 2 and 3**, then read all three together: do you know everyone, and want the next one?
6. **Make episode 1 only.** Voice, direct, render, and you judge it.
7. **Show it to 5 people who don't know the project.** Ask: what was funny, what is each person like, and can you give another example of the mistake? Fix, then make the rest.
8. **After publishing**, watch the share who stayed, the average percentage viewed and the comments. If people name the mistake or tag a friend, the idea works.
