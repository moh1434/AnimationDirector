# Benny, Berry, Wendy and Cherry

They share a home in the present day. The series starts on the day Benny opens his restaurant. Benny is a chef who runs a cloud restaurant from their kitchen, Berry is an independent artist who runs her own small creative business from home, Wendy makes indie games in the garage, and Cherry goes to elementary school. Work and family share the same rooms, so one gets into the other in small ways. The family story lives in what happens between them over the meal, on the way to school, or while someone waits outside the garage.

The relationships below guide new writing. Small moments offered as examples are possibilities for a scene. Only the [series log](SERIES_LOG.md) records which events have happened on screen.

## How the parents earn a living

- **Benny, chef with a cloud restaurant.** He cooks at home for delivery only, so there is no dining room. Customers find him through his social media page. A delivery guy he knows takes the food to them on a delivery motorcycle.
- **Berry, independent artist and creative entrepreneur.** She makes original oil paintings (portraits, landscapes and decorative pieces) and designs handmade jewelry: necklaces, bracelets, earrings and unique accessories. She sells them online through her own social media page.

The delivery guy has no name or voice yet. He wears an orange jacket and an orange helmet and rides a blue motorcycle (props `delivery_rider` with Benny's food, `delivery_rider_art` with Berry's parcels). Record him in the series log once an approved episode shows him.

## The four voices

**Benny (`benny`), 31, father.** Warm, generous, easily taken with a plan. He wants the people he loves to have something to look forward to. He might promise an outing while everyone is still smiling, before checking whether he can go. Embarrassment makes him talk more. A joke is often his first offer of peace. Sometimes his daughter laughs and still needs him to answer her question. Give him the patience to get there. He likes being the parent who remembers the little things. At work he cooks to make people happy and finds it hard to say no to a customer, so he can give away time that belongs to the family. He takes a bad review personally and hides it behind a joke.

**Berry (`berry`), 30, mother.** Observant, direct, imaginative. She can make an ordinary afternoon feel like an occasion. She shows care through preparations, which means discovering someone wants a different plan can sting after she has done the work. She often remembers the last time a person needed something and takes a while to see they have grown. In private she can admit hurt quite plainly. Give her room to be silly, tired, proud of her own work, or simply in need of company. As an artist she finds it hard to put a price on a piece or let a favorite one go. She loses track of time while painting: "almost finished" can mean two more hours.

**Wendy (`wendy`), 18, older daughter.** Finished high school and works on her games in the garage. Calm, spare with words, dryly funny. A small test often helps her make sense of a problem. When someone she loves tells her about a bad day, she may start solving it before hearing them out. She wants her work time respected and still wants to be invited when the others go out. Sometimes she's the last one to realize she owes somebody an apology.

**Cherry (`cherry`), about 10, younger daughter.** Plays games with friends and Wendy. Notices who waited, whose turn got skipped and whether someone used the nickname she asked them to stop using. She loves being trusted with a real job. She also wants company when she has nothing useful to offer. She may agree in the moment and find the words to object later. Her diary holds ordinary joys, complaints and occasionally something Wendy said. Writing in it is one way she thinks. Sometimes she just goes back downstairs.

Keep the established visual identities when describing them. Wendy has a dark purple bob with a high side ponytail, a yellow bow, purple cardigan, white collared shirt and dark purple skirt. Benny wears a yellow hoodie, grey cargo trousers and yellow sneakers, with shaggy brown hair.

## Six relationships to write from

| Pair | What feels good between them | Where they can miss each other |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Benny and Berry | They can make each other laugh after both kids are in bed. Each knows when the other has had a hard day. They are proud of each other's work. | He volunteers them for something because he remembers how much fun it was. She remembers who packed everything. Either one might be the person who needs a night off. They share one home and one working day: he interrupts her with things to taste, she takes over the kitchen table he needs, and each secretly thinks their own deadline is the real one. |
| Benny and Wendy | He likes hearing about the thing she finally got working. She can tease him without having to explain the joke. | He tells people her good news before she is ready. To him it is pride. To her it was something she chose to tell her dad in private. |
| Benny and Cherry | He will take her made-up games seriously and let her explain all the rules. | She remembers the precise plan he mentioned in passing. When he offers a bigger outing to make up for missing it, she may still want the original half hour with him. |
| Berry and Wendy | They can work quietly at the same table, each absorbed in making something. | Berry's habit of checking in feels different now that Wendy works. Wendy may still seek her mother's advice on a day when she can choose the moment herself. |
| Berry and Cherry | Cherry sees little things about Berry's day that the others overlook. They enjoy making things side by side. | Berry can prepare a place for Cherry so lovingly that Cherry feels she can hardly turn it down. Cherry can also be the one who assumes Mom will always be available. |
| Wendy and Cherry | A game lets them be teammates despite the age gap. Wendy genuinely values things Cherry catches in a playtest. | Wendy can mistake an invitation to play for an offer to help with work. Cherry can mistake a closed door for a verdict on how much her sister likes her. |

Use these tensions selectively. Give each pair experiences they enjoy and chances to surprise one another. A remembered preference, a private joke, a borrowed sweatshirt returned clean, or a disagreement quietly reopened in the car can carry affection across episodes. Let behavior evolve once the audience has seen it change.

## Keeping the family believable

Children can speak up, go quiet, change their minds and make mistakes. Parents can hold a reasonable boundary even when a child dislikes it. A parent who applied unfair pressure can take responsibility without asking the child to comfort them. An apology may come before trust or good humour returns.

Side characters can have their own lives and wants. Use one when their presence changes what a family member has to decide. Give them a short lower-case ID and record recurring details after approval. Keep the focus on the family's relationship even when part of the day takes place elsewhere.
