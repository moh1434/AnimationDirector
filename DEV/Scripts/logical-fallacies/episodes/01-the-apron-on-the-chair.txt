# TITLE: The Apron on the Chair
# ARC: setup for Cherry recognizing a sequence mistaken for a cause
# LENGTH: about 52 s, 124 spoken words
# WANT: Benny wants to sell the last bun.
# FALLACY 1: False cause | Benny credits his striped apron for the queue that formed after he put it on.

# REAL LIFE: Deciding what helped a sale from the order of events alone.
# CAST: benny, cherry, wendy
# PLACE: café, afternoon

# HOOK OPTIONS
# 1. Nobody touch that apron. — Why protect it?
# 2. One bun left. Where are my stripes? — Can an apron sell it?
# 3. Yesterday this chair earned a tip. — What did it do?
# CHOSEN: 1 (writer's pick for batch review).

# SEE: Benny ties a striped apron on an empty chair facing the window.
benny: Nobody touch that apron.
cherry: Is somebody sitting there?
benny: Yesterday I put it on. Two minutes later the line went outside.
cherry: That's when the bus dropped off Mom's whole art class.
benny: And what did they see in the window? These beautiful stripes.

# SEE: Benny sets the last bun on the seat.
benny: Today the chair wears it. I'd like to sit down for once.
cherry: If this works, do we have to pay the chair?

# SEE: Wendy turns the chair so its plain back faces the window.
wendy: Dad, which way was your apron facing yesterday?
benny: The oven. I was taking the buns out.
wendy: So the kids in the street couldn't even see it. /** Fallacy 1: False cause */
wendy: Just because one thing happened first doesn't mean it caused the next.

# SEE: Benny takes the apron off and offers Cherry the bun.
benny: Fair enough. Anybody actually want the last one?
cherry: Me. But I want to sit with the staff.

# SEE: Cherry pulls up a seat beside the chair.
cherry: He's very quiet on his break.
